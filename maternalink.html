MaternaLink In development

A care-coordination and communication platform for maternity services, designed to help women stay connected to their care team and help care teams hand over safely — built for both NHS and low-connectivity settings.

Pilot and design-partner invitation What it is, and is not

Status statement

Read this first. MaternaLink is in development. An early prototype exists and is not in clinical use. It is non-diagnostic by design and is not a medical device. It has not been assessed, approved or endorsed by the MHRA, NHS England, any NHS trust, any government or any professional body. There are no pilot sites, no clinical partners and no published evidence. Everything below is a design intention, subject to validation, unless it says otherwise. We will update this statement as each gate on the roadmap is passed.

The problem

Outcomes are unequal, and the failures are often failures of listening and handover

United Kingdom

MBRRACE-UK's Saving Lives, Improving Mothers' Care reports have found, year after year, that Black women are several times more likely to die during or after pregnancy than White women, and that women in the most deprived areas face roughly double the risk. [Cite the current MBRRACE-UK report and exact figures before publication.]

The Ockenden (2022) and Kirkup (2022) reviews describe the mechanism: women not listened to, escalation that did not happen, information lost between community and hospital, shift and shift, antenatal and postnatal.

Nigeria

Nigeria carries the largest single share of the world's maternal deaths. [Cite WHO/UNICEF/UNFPA/World Bank Trends in maternal mortality before publication.] The dominant failure modes are the "three delays": deciding to seek care, reaching a facility, and receiving adequate care once there.

Most women outside cities use feature phones. Antenatal contacts are missed and nobody can see, in near-real time, who has fallen out of contact. Education arrives inconsistently and often in the wrong language.

What is common to both

  • A woman's own account of how she feels is the most under-used information in maternity care.
  • Handover between professionals is where information is lost.
  • Education is delivered in the wrong language or at the wrong literacy level.
  • Programmes cannot see who has missed a contact and who is chasing them.

None of this is solved by software alone. But each of these is a coordination problem, and coordination is something software can help with safely.

Our approach

We re-based the product from prediction to coordination. Here is why.

MaternaLink began in 2025 as a concept for a multimodal AI "Maternal Instability Score" that would predict complications from clinical, voice and social data. That concept had no data, no model, no validation and no clinical partner behind it — and, as a prediction tool, it would almost certainly be a medical device requiring years of validation before any claim could be made.

So version 1 is deliberately narrower. It is intended to support communication, education, appointment adherence and information handover between women and their care teams, and to support programme administration. It is designed to support, not replace, clinical judgement.

The original AI concept is not discarded. It is held as a research track — version 3 and beyond — with explicit prerequisites: data partnerships, ethics approval, retrospective and prospective validation, fairness analysis by ethnicity and deprivation, and a UK MDR / MHRA software-as-a-medical-device pathway. It is presented to investors as an option with a cost and a timeline, not as a product on the roadmap.

"MaternaLink (in development) is a software platform intended to support communication, education, appointment scheduling and information handover between women receiving maternity care and their care teams, and to support programme administration. It is not intended to diagnose, prevent, monitor, predict, prognose, treat or alleviate any disease, injury or physiological condition, nor to provide clinical decision support. Information recorded by users is displayed without automated interpretation for review by qualified professionals who remain responsible for all clinical decisions."

Proposed intended-use statement, version 1. To be validated with regulatory counsel against MHRA guidance on software as a medical device before any deployment.

Scope

What MaternaLink is, and what it is not

Is designed to be

  • A care-coordination and communication platform for maternity services
  • Non-diagnostic: information is displayed, never interpreted by the system
  • A support for handover between community, hospital, shifts and stages of care
  • A channel for clinically signed-off education in the right language and literacy level
  • Offline-capable and usable over two-way SMS, for feature phones and poor connectivity
  • Built with a Clinical Safety Officer, a hazard log and a DPIA from the start
  • In development, subject to validation, with a published roadmap and gates

Is not and will not be claimed to be

  • A medical device, or a diagnostic, triage, prediction or risk-scoring tool
  • NHS-approved, DTAC-assessed, MHRA-registered, UKCA- or CE-marked
  • Clinically validated, or shown to reduce maternal mortality or morbidity
  • An electronic patient record; the organisation's record system remains the record
  • An emergency service: every screen states where to go and whom to call in an emergency
  • A chatbot, symptom checker or source of generated advice
  • A partner of, or endorsed by, any NHS body, government or professional body

Who it is for

Designed around six kinds of user

Women, and with consent a birth companion

Smartphone users in the UK; feature-phone users across much of Nigeria; English plus community languages, with Yoruba first on the roadmap. Companion access is a version-2 item with safeguarding controls.

Community midwives and community health workers

Registered midwives in the UK; midwives, community health extension workers and other cadres in Nigeria, with reduced permissions for non-registered staff.

Hospital clinicians

Obstetricians, maternity triage and anaesthetists who receive a handover summary rather than a phone call from memory.

Service and programme managers

Heads of midwifery, digital midwives and quality leads in the UK; state maternal-health desks and primary-care agencies in Nigeria.

NGO and donor programme officers

People who need to know who has missed a contact, who is chasing them, and an export they can trust for a logframe.

Operators and administrators

Trust IT and information governance; state IT; implementing partners. Vytalix support access is break-glass only and fully audited.

Roadmap

Twenty-four months, with gates that can be checked

Dates are proposed, not promised. Each gate is something an outsider could verify. We will mark each item as it is passed and say plainly if it slips.

  1. Gate 1 · Q4 2026Ownership, audit, safety roles

    IP assigned to the company; prototype audited; Clinical Safety Officer appointed; hazard log opened; DPIA started; ICO registration. Discovery interviews: 20 women and 10 professionals per market.

  2. Q1 2027Minimum viable product complete

    Twelve to sixteen weeks of build; internal alpha; SMS channel; penetration test; clinical safety case version 1; DTAC readiness pack; Cyber Essentials.

  3. Gate 2 · Q2 2027UK service evaluation, unpaid

    One community midwifery team under the trust's own clinical governance (DCB0160), with an agreed protocol. Nigeria pilot designed with an NGO or state partner.

  4. Q3 2027Nigeria pilot

    One or two local government areas, 500–2,000 women, through an implementing partner, with ethics approval for the evaluation. USSD and DHIS2 export.

  5. Gate 3 · Q4 2027Evidence and the first device decision

    UK feasibility study begins. Rule-based flagging goes through a documented device qualification decision; if it is a device, the Class I route begins before release.

  6. 2028Version 2 and the research option

    Integration, partner access, localised libraries. Formal UK evaluation. Only with data-sharing agreements and ethics approval: retrospective modelling for the version-3 research track.

What we will report

Operational and safety measures, honestly, each period: messages acknowledged within the agreed time; unresolved escalations; content incidents; contacts completed against schedule; defaulters traced within seven days; uptime against a 99.5% design target; accessibility audit results.

Never reported as product outcomes without a formal evaluation: maternal deaths, severe morbidity, or "lives saved".

The research track, stated carefully

The version-3 research track would require, in order: a data partnership with at least one NHS trust or academic maternity unit and, for Nigeria, a teaching hospital, under ethics approval; retrospective model development with a pre-registered protocol and fairness analysis; MHRA qualification and classification, probably Class IIa or higher; prospective validation in silent mode; and only then any clinical claim, proportionate to the evidence. Our estimate is 24–36 months and £1.5m–£4m. It is an option, not a commitment.

Safety and data principles

Design targets we build to — not certifications we hold

Each item below is how MaternaLink is being designed. None is a claim of current compliance. Where a standard or certificate is named, it is the target we will evidence before deployment.

  • Clinical safety A registered clinician as Clinical Safety Officer from the start; a hazard log and clinical safety case under DCB0129; deploying NHS organisations complete DCB0160. Hazards already listed include an unread urgent message, the wrong woman on a handover summary, a stale schedule after a due-date change, and a diary value mistaken for a clinical assessment.
  • Content governance Two-person sign-off on every educational item (clinical author plus reviewer), sources cited, version and review date shown, a content-incident route.
  • A human owns every escalation Every safety-relevant flag has a named owner and a response time; unresolved items climb the rota; the trail is audited.
  • Safeguarding Discreet mode for shared phones and coercive situations, no partner access in version 1, a woman-initiated "I am not safe" route to a named human, under-18 and capacity workflows aligned to local policy. The system never assesses capacity or risk.
  • Clinical advisory group Three to five members — obstetrician, senior midwife, GP or public-health physician, Nigerian obstetrician or midwife, lived-experience representative — to be appointed and named only with consent. None is appointed today.
  • Data minimisation Each feature collects only what it needs; profile fields are per role; no free-text clinical history from women beyond "things I want my team to know".
  • Roles and lawful basis In NHS deployments the trust is controller and Vytalix is processor under a data-processing agreement. A DPIA is completed before any live data. UK data is designed to stay in a UK region; a Nigerian deployment is designed as a separate cell with in-country or nearby hosting under NDPA 2023 safeguards.
  • No training on patient data No patient data is used to train or fine-tune any model, ours or a vendor's, without ethics approval, a DPIA and, where applicable, explicit consent — enforced by policy, contract and architecture.
  • Security Design targets: multi-factor authentication for staff, passcode and quick-hide for women, per-organisation isolation, encryption in transit and at rest, field-level encryption of identifiers, audit of every access, annual penetration testing, Cyber Essentials Plus, DSPT "Standards Met", and 72-hour breach-notification readiness.
  • Rights and retention Subject access, rectification and deletion supported by tooling, subject to record-keeping duties. Retention set by the controller; the organisation's record system remains the record of care.
Pilot invitation

We are looking for design partners, not customers

The most valuable thing MaternaLink can have in its first year is one real service willing to co-design and evaluate it honestly. We are looking for three kinds of partner.

  • A UK maternity service — one trust, one community midwifery team, with a digital midwife and a Clinical Safety Officer willing to run a three-to-six-month service evaluation under DCB0160. No fee. We bring the safety case, the DPIA template, the assurance artefacts and the support.
  • A Nigerian implementing partner — an established NGO or state primary-care programme with an existing antenatal cohort, willing to run scheduled two-way SMS and defaulter tracing with a defined group of women, under ethics approval for the evaluation. Where a state already has a programme, we aim to complement it, not compete.
  • A clinical academic partner — a maternity research unit willing to design the service evaluation and, later, a feasibility study, and to publish whatever the results are.

What we ask of a partner

  • A named clinical lead and an information-governance contact
  • A defined cohort and a written evaluation protocol agreed before go-live
  • Honest feedback, including "stop"
  • Publication rights for both sides, whatever the findings

What we promise

  • No clinical claims, in any material, during or after the pilot
  • No fee until there is evidence to price against
  • Termination for safety by either side at any time
  • Your name is used only with written consent
