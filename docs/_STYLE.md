# HOUSE STYLE — every Vytalix document follows this

The reader is a solo founder who is not a doctor, not a lawyer and not a software engineer. He is busy. He found the first version of these documents overwhelming. Write so that he can read one document in ten minutes and know what to do.

## Structure every document must use

```
# <Number> — <Plain title, no jargon>

**One line saying what this document is for.**

## In one minute
Three to five bullets. The whole document in the simplest words possible.
Each bullet one sentence. No sentence longer than 20 words.

## What to do
A short table: Action | Who | By when. Five rows at most.

## <The detail, in short sections>
Short sections with clear headings. Tables for anything with numbers.
A diagram where one helps.

## Words explained
Only if the document had to use technical words. Two columns: Word | What it means.
```

Every document ends there. No "Priorities / Risks / Next actions" block any more; the "What to do" table at the top replaces it.

## Language rules

1. **Short sentences.** One idea each. Aim for 15 to 20 words. Never more than 25.
2. **No consultancy words.** Banned: leverage, ecosystem, value proposition, stakeholder, synergy, go-to-market, commercialisation, de-risk, optionality, holistic, robust, best-in-class, world-class, cutting-edge, seamless, enable, utilise, facilitate, deliverable, workstream, granular, actionable, drive (as a verb for people), unlock, journey (unless it is a patient's actual care), space (meaning market), play (meaning strategy), motion, north star, double-click.
3. **Say the plain word.** Use "money" not "capital" where it fits. "Buy" not "procure". "Use" not "utilise". "Start" not "initiate". "Help" not "facilitate". "About" not "approximately". "Now" not "at this juncture".
4. **Explain every technical term the first time, in brackets.** For example: "DTAC (the NHS checklist a digital product must pass before a hospital can buy it)". "SEIS (a tax scheme that gives people money back when they invest in a small British company)".
5. **Numbers go in tables, not in sentences.** One number in a sentence is fine. Three is not.
6. **Active voice.** "You sign the agreement", not "the agreement should be signed".
7. **Say who does what.** Never "it is recommended that". Say "do this" or "ask your solicitor to do this".
8. **Keep the honesty.** Keep every label: KNOWN, VERIFIED, ASSUMPTION, ESTIMATE, TO VALIDATE, PROPOSED. But explain them in plain words the first time, for example: "ESTIMATE (our best guess, not a fact)".
9. **No emoji. No exclamation marks.**
10. **Length.** 200 lines maximum per document. If it will not fit, cut detail, do not shrink the type.

## Truth rules that never change
- Nothing claims a customer, partner, approval, clinician, revenue or pilot that does not exist.
- MaternaLink is "in development". It is not approved, validated or assessed by anyone.
- The £300 per woman per year figure from the March 2026 Ekiti proposal is withdrawn.
- The 2025 AI prediction deck is withdrawn from external use.
- Never say NHS-approved, clinically proven, CE or UKCA marked, or government partner.
