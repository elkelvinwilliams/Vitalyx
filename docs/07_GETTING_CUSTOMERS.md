## The conversation
A discovery call is a first 30-minute call to find out what someone needs. Keep to this shape.

| Minutes | What you do |
|---|---|
| 0 to 3 | Thank them, agree the agenda, ask permission to ask questions |
| 3 to 8 | Their role, their organisation, what made them take the call |
| 8 to 18 | The problem: what happens today, what they have tried, what it costs them |
| 18 to 23 | The decision: budget, who signs, timing, what success looks like |
| 23 to 27 | Say back what you heard; describe one or two ways you could help. No hard sell |
| 27 to 30 | Agree one specific next step and a date |

The eight questions worth asking:
1. What made you take this call now?
2. How is this handled today, and what have you already tried?
3. What does success look like for you in six months?
4. What is this costing you now, in time, money or risk?
5. Who signs off on money like this, and is there a budget already?
6. What is setting the timing: a board meeting, a funding round, a deadline?
7. What would make this a no?
8. If we started on a set date, what would the first two weeks need to deliver?

Write the answers into the CRM the same day, under: problem, cost, who decides, budget, timing, what success means, next step.

## The ten objections and what to say

| They say | You say |
|---|---|
| "You are too new, you have no track record." | "True, and I will not pretend otherwise. What I bring is [your real experience]. The work is fixed-scope and paid on delivery, and the first piece is small. If it does not land, you stop." |
| "Too expensive." | "Compared with what? If it is in-house time, let us cost that. If it is budget, Option A is scoped to [price] and the rest can wait." |
| "No budget this quarter." | "Understood. Would a smaller piece at [price] that builds your business case for next quarter help? Otherwise let us put a date in for [month]." |
| "We can do this ourselves." | "You can. The question is speed. We would compress weeks of learning. If you would rather build the skill in-house, the workshop version hands the method to your team." |
| "Send me some information." | "Happy to. So I send the right thing: is the priority [A] or [B]? I will send a one-pager and suggest 15 minutes to talk it through." |
| "We are talking to a bigger firm." | "Good, and compare us on who actually does the work, on speed and on price. I deliver it myself, and I will put that in writing." |
| "Is MaternaLink live? Can we see it?" | "MaternaLink is in development. There is an early prototype and I will show it as a concept, not a product. Today is about the advisory work, which does not depend on it." |
| "Are you NHS-approved or CE-marked?" | "No, and we do not claim to be. Advisory work does not need it. Any software going into the NHS would go through DTAC and clinical safety first, which is the path we help clients plan." |
| "It is not the right time." | "When would be? What changes by then? If [trigger] happens sooner, would you want to move faster?" |
| "Can you do it for free, or for equity?" | "I will give you a free 30 minutes. Paid work stays paid, because that is how I stay independent. Equity only alongside cash, and only where it fits." |

## How to price a proposal, and when to walk away
Keep the proposal to about four pages: what you understood, what success means, what you will do, when, the price, what you assume, and a signature line. Offer two or three options and put a date on which the price expires.

| Rule | The number |
|---|---|
| Day rate | £900, range £800 to £1,200 (ESTIMATE) |
| Monthly retainer | £2,500 to £5,000 |
| Fixed sprint | £7,500 to £25,000 |
| Never go below | £650 a day equivalent |
| Discount | 15% at most, and only for prepayment or volume. Never cut price without cutting scope or getting something back, such as a reference |
| Payment | Projects 50% up front. Retainers monthly in advance |
| Free work | One free 30-minute session per prospect. No free workshops |
| MaternaLink pilots | Never free. Minimum £10,000 (ESTIMATE), and only with data-protection and safety conditions agreed first |
| Nigerian government work | Quote in pounds or dollars. At least 30% paid before you start. No work before that payment arrives |

Walk away when any of these is true: they ask you to go below the floor; they want free work or equity with no cash; after two calls nobody will name who signs; they want you to say something untrue, such as NHS-approved or clinically proven; the scope keeps growing while the price stays still.

## Words explained
| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| CRM | The software that holds your contacts, your deals and what stage each one is at |
| Discovery call | A first 30-minute call to find out what someone needs, before any pitch |
| Retainer | A fixed monthly fee for an agreed amount of your time |
| Sprint | A fixed piece of work with a fixed price and an end date |
| DTAC | The NHS checklist a digital product must pass before a hospital can buy it |
| Inbound | Someone who contacts you first, rather than you contacting them |
| Mobilisation payment | Money paid up front so you can start work, common in government contracts |
