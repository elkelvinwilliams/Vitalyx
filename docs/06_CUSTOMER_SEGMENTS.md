# 06 — VYTALIX CUSTOMER SEGMENTS, ICP AND TARGET-ACCOUNT ARCHETYPES

**Owner:** Chief Strategy Officer · **Status:** PROPOSED v1 · **Date:** 8 September 2026 · **Governing file:** `/FACTS_BASE.md` (KNOWN / VERIFIED / ASSUMPTION / ESTIMATE / PROPOSED / TO VALIDATE apply throughout). Vytalix has no customers, pilots, partners or references in any segment today (KNOWN). Every budget, sales-cycle and price figure below is an ESTIMATE from desk research and sector experience unless a URL is given; each must be tested in discovery calls before it is used in a proposal. Prices reference the service catalogue in `/docs/01_BUSINESS_MODEL.md` §5 (codes C1–C8, A1–A2, T1, E1).

## Executive view

1. **Eighteen segments were examined (13 B2B, 5 B2C); only four can pay Vytalix within 90 days**: health-tech companies (UK and inbound), NGOs and development programmes with live budget lines, private healthcare groups/clinics, and universities/accelerators buying training. NHS trusts, ICBs, pharma, insurers and governments are 6–18-month cycles that a solo, unincorporated founder cannot close this year — they are relationship-building segments in 2026 and revenue segments in 2027–28.
2. **The ideal customer profile (ICP) for the services business is a UK or inbound digital-health company, pre-seed to Series A, with an NHS or African go-to-market problem and a founder who decides in a week.** It scores 89/100 on the ICP model in §3 versus 46/100 for NHS trusts, because it combines short cycle, founder-to-founder access, a fee (£7.5k–£22k, ESTIMATE) that needs no procurement, and direct learning for MaternaLink (in development).
3. **MaternaLink's buyers and MaternaLink's users are different segments with different economics.** Buyers are trusts, private maternity units, state ministries via donors, NGOs and (later) insurers/HMOs; users are expectant mothers, families, midwives and community health workers. B2C segments are therefore treated as *design and adoption* segments, not revenue segments, except for E-Learning where individual professionals pay £49–£299 per course (ESTIMATE).
4. **Ten target-account archetypes (§4) — not ten named companies — define the first 90 days of outbound.** Each archetype has a trigger that can be observed publicly (a funding announcement, a CQC rating, a tender, a grant call), an entry offer, and a decision-maker title. Named accounts are populated in the CRM only from public sources and only after the archetype is validated by three conversations.
5. **The Ekiti/Nigeria government segment is deliberately ranked low for 2026 revenue** despite being the only live conversation: the state has a funded incumbent (mDoc / MSD for Mothers, VERIFIED), pricing is unvalidated, and government payment risk is high. It stays in the plan as a partnership and evidence segment (see `/docs/12_PARTNERSHIP_STRATEGY.md`), with NGO-funded cohorts as the commercial route.

## 0. Method and definitions

| Term | Meaning in this document |
|---|---|
| Segment | A group of organisations or people who share a buying problem, a decision-maker type and a budget source. |
| Pillar | Which Vytalix division sells to the segment: **Advisory** (founder-led days/retainers), **Consulting** (fixed-price sprints C1–C8), **Health Solutions** (MaternaLink, in development — pilots only), **E-Learning** (courses, licences). |
| Budget (ESTIMATE) | The realistic first-engagement value Vytalix could win from a single account in the next 12–24 months; not the segment's total spend. |
| Sales cycle | First contact to signed order, ESTIMATE, assuming no prior relationship. |
| Horizon | **Now** (invoice within 90 days of incorporation); **6m**; **12m**; **24m+**. |
| Objections | The three most likely reasons the buyer says no, phrased as the buyer would say them; responses live in `/docs/13_SALES_ENGINE.md` §15. |

Segment ranking uses the ICP model in §3 (six weighted criteria). All scores are desk ESTIMATES to be re-scored after the first 40 discovery conversations.

### 2.2 Expectant mothers (UK and Nigeria/Kenya)

## 3. ICP ranking

### 3.1 Scoring model (PROPOSED)

Six criteria, weights sum to 100; each scored 1–5 (5 = most favourable); ICP score = Σ(weight × score)/5.

| Criterion | Weight | What "5" means |
|---|---|---|
| Speed to cash (sales cycle, procurement) | 25 | Signs in < 6 weeks without procurement |
| Budget accessibility (first-engagement value vs effort) | 20 | £10k–£40k obtainable from one decision maker |
| Founder reach (can a solo founder access the decision maker now?) | 20 | Direct, founder-to-founder or via LinkedIn/referral |
| Strategic value to MaternaLink (learning, evidence, references) | 15 | Directly shapes or de-risks the product or its route to market |
| Competitive intensity (room for a boutique) | 10 | Few credible alternatives at Vytalix's price point |
| Regulatory/compliance burden to serve | 10 | No patient data, no assurance artefacts needed |

### 3.3 ICP statement for the services business (PROPOSED)

> A digital-health company — UK-based or entering the UK or an African market — between pre-seed and Series A, with 5–50 staff, that has raised or is raising money in the last or next six months, whose founder or COO owns the go-to-market problem (NHS entry, assurance, evidence, grants, investor narrative, or Nigeria/Kenya entry), and who can sign a £7.5k–£22k fixed-price engagement or a £2.5k–£6k monthly retainer without a procurement process.
