# 19 — The twelve organisations on MamaDoc's wall

**Who they are, what each is worth to you, and which ones you must not go near.**

## In one minute

- These are MamaDoc's supporters, not yours. You have no relationship with any of them.
- The pattern is the lesson: MamaDoc built a base in one city, then added national charity names on top.
- Six are fair to approach directly. Three are MamaDoc's home turf in Leicester. Leave those alone.
- Five X More is the one to be most careful with. Never approach them as a vendor.
- Leicester City Council is the surprise. Councils buy maternity support, and faster than trusts do.

## The twelve

### National maternity charities

| Who | What they are | Worth to you |
|---|---|---|
| **Tommy's** | The largest UK pregnancy and baby charity. Funds research, runs clinics | Sets what counts as evidence in maternity. A content and research route |
| **NCT** | The biggest antenatal education charity in the country | Defines what parents expect antenatal support to be. Content licensing, possibly |
| **Birthrights** | Human rights in childbirth. Published the inquiry into racism in maternity care | **The most valuable name here for your specific claim.** If they say your consent and records design is right, that is worth more than any testimonial |
| **Bliss** | Premature and sick babies | Only matters once the newborn module is real. Park it |
| **MAMA Academy** | Safer pregnancy charity, known for maternity notes wallets and symptom awareness | Very close to your escalation argument. Small and practical, likely to reply |
| **For Baby's Sake** | Works with both parents where there is domestic abuse | MBRRACE found 21% of the women who died had experienced domestic abuse. Ask them a question you cannot answer |

## The rule about the Leicester four

You met Yusuf today. If you spend next week contacting the organisations whose logos sit on
his website, he will hear about it, because maternity in one city is a small world. It would
cost you a relationship worth more than any of those introductions.

National charities work with everyone, so they are not his to gatekeep. The Leicester
organisations are his base. Leave them. If a relationship there ever makes sense, it comes
through him, later, and with his knowledge.

## What this wall actually teaches you

Look at the shape of it rather than the names. MamaDoc did three things in order.

1. **Won one city first.** Four of the twelve are Leicester organisations, including the council.
   Depth in one place, not a thin national launch.
2. **Borrowed credibility.** Tommy's, NCT, Birthrights and Bliss are names a hospital already
   trusts. A small organisation gets taken seriously by standing next to them.
3. **Stayed specific.** Every name on that wall serves Black, Brown, migrant or vulnerable
   parents. Nothing is there by accident.

You have been trying to start nationally with no city, no charity names and no proof. That is
harder, not more ambitious. Pick a city. It does not have to be Leicester, and it probably
should not be.

## How to approach a charity, if you do

Charities are asked for things constantly and offered very little. So ask a question, do not
make an offer.

> "I'm building maternity software and I have a problem I cannot solve from the outside.
> [The specific problem.] You have seen this for years and I have not. Could I have twenty
> minutes, or could you point me at what I should read first?"

Never ask a charity for a testimonial, a logo, or an introduction to a hospital on first
contact. They protect their name carefully, and rightly.

## Words explained

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Family Hubs | Council-run centres for families with young children, with money attached |
| Patient involvement group | A panel of real patients who tell you whether a design works before you build it |
| Asset transfer | When a council hands a building over to a community organisation to run |
| Peer supporter | A mother trained to support other mothers. Not a clinician, and often trusted more |
