Who we advise

Five kinds of organisation, one common question: "will this work in real care?"

  • Health-tech founders, pre-seed to Series A who need to know which NHS door to knock on, whether their product is a medical device, and what a grant reviewer will ask.
  • Investors, accelerators and incubators who want portfolio companies stress-tested on regulatory pathway, evidence plan and NHS readiness before the next round.
  • NHS-adjacent innovation teams — ICB digital leads, trust innovation offices, Health Innovation Networks and maternity charities — weighing up a digital maternity or health-inequalities initiative.
  • African ministries of health and development partners designing or evaluating a digital maternal-health programme and needing an independent view before they commit.
  • Employers and insurers with a women's-health agenda who want a credible programme design rather than a vendor pitch.
  • Founders of Vytalix's own kind — people trying to build something safe in maternity care. We share what we have learned, including our mistakes.
What we advise on

Six subjects, each with a concrete outcome

Every advisory engagement ends in a written note you can act on or hand to your board. These are the questions we are most often asked, and what you leave with.

Strategy and positioning

Which problem you actually solve, for whom, and what the buyer already spends on it. Outcome: a one-page positioning statement and a list of claims you can and cannot make today.

NHS market entry

How ICBs, trusts and national bodies buy; where DTAC, DCB0129, DSPT and Cyber Essentials sit; realistic sales cycles. Outcome: a buyer map and an assurance checklist ordered by what blocks you first.

Regulatory pathway

Whether your intended purpose makes the software a medical device under UK MDR 2002, what an MHRA qualification decision needs, and how to stay outside scope on purpose. Outcome: a draft intended-use statement and a line-crossing feature list. Not legal advice; we tell you when to instruct counsel.

Data protection and clinical safety

Controller and processor roles, DPIA triggers, special-category conditions, the Clinical Safety Officer role. Outcome: a processing inventory and a hazard-log starter for your product.

Funding readiness

Grant fit (Innovate UK, NIHR i4i, SBRI Healthcare, foundation calls), SEIS/EIS considerations, what a health-tech angel will probe. Outcome: a grant calendar and a deck review with the weak claims marked.

Nigeria and wider African market entry

Federal and state structures, donor-funded programmes, incumbents to complement rather than fight, NDPA 2023 data rules. Outcome: a country scorecard and an entry model recommendation.

How an engagement runs

From first call to written note

  1. A 30-minute conversation, free. You describe the problem. We say whether we are the right people, and if not, who might be.
  2. A one-page brief. The questions we will answer, what you will give us to read, the price, and what "done" looks like. Nothing starts until you have agreed it in writing.
  3. The work. Reading, interviews if needed, working sessions with you. We tell you early if the answer is going to be uncomfortable.
  4. A written note within five working days. Findings, recommendation, the trade-offs we rejected, and the claims you can now make. Short enough to be read; sourced enough to be trusted.
  5. One follow-up call, included. Usually four to six weeks later, to see what happened and adjust.

What advisory is not. It is not legal, regulatory, accounting or clinical advice, and it is not a certification of anything. Where a decision needs a solicitor, a regulatory consultant, an accountant or a registered clinician, we say so and, where we can, introduce one. We never sign off a clinical safety case or a DPIA as if we were your Clinical Safety Officer or DPO.

Conflicts, disclosed. Vytalix is developing MaternaLink, a maternity care-coordination platform. If your product or programme could compete with or buy from MaternaLink, we tell you before the brief is written, and you decide whether to proceed.
