Product principles

Eight rules that decide what we build and how

These are the tests any Vytalix product must pass before code is written, and again before anything is deployed. They are deliberately restrictive.

  1. Say what it is, on the first screen. Every product states its stage, intended use and limits where users can see them — "in development", "non-diagnostic", "not a medical device" — not in a footer.
  2. Non-diagnostic by default. We build for coordination, communication, education and handover first. Anything that interprets data about an individual for a medical purpose goes through a documented qualification decision and, if it is a device, the UK MDR pathway before release. We do not cross that line by accident.
  3. Safety case before code. A Clinical Safety Officer, a hazard log under DCB0129 and a data protection impact assessment are opened before the build starts, not retrofitted for a buyer.
  4. A human owns every escalation. The system can route, remind and record. It never decides. Every safety-relevant flag has a named person and a response time.
  1. Collect only what a feature needs. Data minimisation is a design constraint. No patient data is used to train any model, ours or a vendor's, without ethics approval, a DPIA and, where required, explicit consent.
  2. Built for low connectivity. Offline-capable web, two-way SMS and low-data content are first-class channels, because a product that only works on a smartphone with 4G excludes the women most at risk.
  3. Open standards, no lock-in. FHIR UK Core, DHIS2-compatible exports and plain CSV. The organisation's record system remains the record; we do not try to replace it.
  4. Measured honestly. We report operational metrics — messages acknowledged in time, contacts completed, defaulters traced — and never claim clinical outcomes without a formal evaluation.
Our first product

MaternaLink In development

A care-coordination and communication platform for maternity services, designed to help women stay connected to their care team and help care teams hand over safely — built for both NHS and low-connectivity settings.

It is designed to support enrolment and consent, contact schedules and reminders, a clinically signed-off education library, secure messaging with human ownership, a self-recorded diary that is displayed rather than interpreted, a care-team console, structured handover summaries, an escalation log and missed-contact tracing.

It does not diagnose, predict, triage or score. It is not a medical device, and it has not been assessed by the MHRA or any NHS body.

What we are not building

Three things you will not find in a Vytalix product until they are validated

Automated risk scores

The multimodal "maternal instability" concept that started this company is a research track, not a feature. It needs data partnerships, ethics approval, prospective validation and an MHRA software-as-a-medical-device pathway before it can make any clinical claim.

Symptom checkers and chatbot advice

Anything shown to a woman is pre-approved, clinically signed-off content with a named author and review date. Nothing is generated on the fly for her.

Threshold alerts on her observations

A blood-pressure reading she records is displayed to her care team, not interpreted. Rule-based flagging against clinician-set parameters is a version-2 candidate that goes through a device qualification decision first.

How an idea becomes a product

Four gates, in order, before anything is called a product

  1. Ownership and audit. The intellectual property is assigned to the company in writing, and any existing prototype is audited for code quality, security and data handling before a line is reused.
  2. Safety and data governance. A Clinical Safety Officer is appointed, the hazard log is opened, the DPIA is started, the intended-use statement is written, and the company is registered with the ICO.
  3. Design partner. A real service — a community midwifery team, an NGO cohort — agrees in writing to co-design and evaluate under its own clinical governance. No fee is charged for this.
  4. Evidence. A service evaluation with a protocol, then a feasibility study, then formal evaluation. Claims are limited to what each step shows.

A second product?

Not yet. We will not announce a second product until it has passed the first gate. Ideas we are watching — non-clinical uses of AI for message routing by type, translation drafts reviewed by staff, operational summaries for programme managers — are extensions of MaternaLink, and each one is listed on its roadmap with its own guardrails.
